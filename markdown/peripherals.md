# PC Building Guide: Peripherals (Keyboards, Mice, Headsets & More)

## What peripherals do

Peripherals are the devices you use to control and hear the computer: the keyboard, mouse and headset, plus extras like a mousepad, microphone and webcam. They don't affect FPS, but they have a large effect on **comfort, accuracy and enjoyment**, and you touch them every second you use the PC. Good peripherals often last through several PC builds, so they're a sensible place to spend a little more. For comfort, **fit and feel matter more than specs**, so try devices in person when possible.

**Common specs across all peripherals:**

| Spec | What it means | Why it matters |
|---|---|---|
| **Wired vs. wireless** | A cable, a **2.4GHz dongle** or **Bluetooth**. | Modern 2.4GHz wireless is as fast as wired for gaming. Bluetooth has more lag and is better for office use or connecting to several devices. |
| **Polling rate (Hz)** | How often the device reports to the PC: 125 / 1000 / 4000 / 8000Hz. | 1000Hz is the standard. **8K polling** cuts delay slightly for competitive players but uses more CPU and battery. |
| **Software** | The companion app for remapping keys, lighting, EQ settings and profiles. | Good software adds features, and bad software can be bloated. Onboard memory lets settings go with the device. |
| **Build & warranty** | Materials, cable quality, switch lifespan. | Cheap devices wear out fast. A 2-year+ warranty is a good sign. |

## Keyboards

### Switch types

| Type | How it works | Feel | Pros | Cons |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Membrane** | Rubber dome presses a contact | Mushy, quiet | Cheap, spill-resistant | Less precise, wears out, feels vague |
| **Scissor / laptop-style** | Low-profile membrane with scissor mechanism | Short, crisp | Slim, quiet, good for typing | Little customization |
| **Mechanical** | Individual spring switch per key | Varies by switch | Durable, satisfying, customizable, hot-swappable | Louder, pricier |
| **Hall effect / magnetic** | Magnet + sensor measures key position | Smooth linear | **Rapid Trigger**, adjustable actuation, no wear on contacts | Most expensive, gaming-focused |
| **Optical** | Light beam detects key press | Varies | Very fast, durable | Fewer switch/keycap options |

**Mechanical switch feels:**
- **Linear** (like Red switches): smooth straight down. Popular for gaming.
- **Tactile** (like Brown switches): a bump you can feel when the key registers. Good for typing and a good all-rounder.
- **Clicky** (like Blue switches): a bump plus an audible click. Satisfying but loud, so not ideal for shared spaces or voice chat.

**Hall effect keyboards** are the big trend in 2026. The keyboard measures exactly how far each key is pressed, which allows:
- **Adjustable actuation:** you set how far down a key has to go before it registers, from about 0.1mm to 4mm.
- **Rapid Trigger:** a key resets the moment you start lifting it, which makes strafing in competitive shooters faster.
- **Analog input:** some boards can read keys like a controller joystick.

### Keyboard sizes (form factors)

| Size | What's included | Best for |
|---|---|---|
| **Full-size (100%)** | Everything, including the number pad | Office work, spreadsheets, data entry |
| **96% / 1800** | Full layout squeezed together | Keeps the number pad in less space |
| **TKL (80%)** | No number pad | **Best all-rounder**, more room for the mouse |
| **75%** | Compact, keeps the F-row and arrows | Popular enthusiast size |
| **65%** | No F-row, keeps the arrow keys | Gamers with low-sensitivity aim who want mouse room |
| **60%** | No F-row, no arrow keys | Minimalist and competitive gaming. Has a learning curve. |

**Other features:** hot-swappable switches (change switches without soldering), PBT keycaps (more durable and less shiny than ABS), gasket mounting (softer and quieter typing), a volume knob, and a wrist rest.

## Mice

### What differs between models

| Spec | What it means | Why it matters |
|---|---|---|
| **Sensor** | The optical sensor that tracks movement. | Any modern flagship sensor is effectively flawless. Avoid no-name budget sensors. |
| **DPI / CPI** | Sensitivity, or how far the cursor moves per inch. | Big DPI numbers (30,000+) are mostly marketing. Most players use **400–1600 DPI**. |
| **Weight** | From ultralight (under 60g) to heavy (over 100g). | Lighter mice are easier to flick and less tiring for aiming. Some people prefer a heavier, steadier mouse. |
| **Shape** | **Ergonomic** (right-handed, sculpted) or **symmetrical** (ambidextrous). | **The most important factor.** It has to fit your hand size and grip. |
| **Buttons** | Standard 5 buttons, or 12+ for MMO mice. | More buttons help in MMOs and productivity. FPS players usually keep it minimal. |
| **Switches** | Mechanical, optical or new **haptic/inductive** clicks. | Optical and haptic switches don't develop "double-click" failures over time. |
| **Feet (skates)** | PTFE (Teflon) glides on the bottom. | Smooth, pure-PTFE feet give a more consistent glide. |

### Grip styles
- **Palm:** your whole hand rests on the mouse. Suits larger or ergonomic mice.
- **Claw:** your palm touches the back and your fingers arch. Suits medium mice with a hump toward the back.
- **Fingertip:** only your fingertips touch the mouse. Suits small, light mice.

## Headsets & audio

### What differs between models

| Spec | What it means | Why it matters |
|---|---|---|
| **Open-back vs. closed-back** | Whether the ear cups are vented or sealed. | **Closed** blocks outside noise and doesn't leak sound, so it's best in shared spaces. **Open** sounds wider and more natural, and is better for positional audio, but leaks sound and blocks nothing. |
| **Drivers** | The speakers inside the ear cups (usually 40–50mm). | Tuning and quality matter more than size. |
| **Microphone** | Boom mic (detachable or flip-up) or built-in. | Headset mics are fine for voice chat, but a separate mic sounds much better for streaming. |
| **Connection** | 3.5mm analog, USB, 2.4GHz wireless or Bluetooth. | 2.4GHz is best for gaming. Bluetooth has lag, so avoid it for games. Many headsets support dual 2.4GHz + Bluetooth. |
| **Surround sound** | Virtual 7.1, Dolby Atmos, DTS:X, or Windows Sonic. | Can help with positional audio, but a good stereo headset is often just as effective. |
| **Comfort** | Weight, clamping force, and pad material (leatherette or velour). | Critical for long sessions. Velour breathes better, and leatherette seals better. |
| **ANC** | Active noise cancelling. | Useful in noisy environments. It's more common on premium models. |

**Alternative setups:**
- **Headphones plus a separate mic:** Audiophile headphones with a USB or clip-on mic often beat a gaming headset at the same price.
- **IEMs (in-ear monitors):** Budget IEMs can deliver excellent, accurate sound for very little money.
- **Speakers:** Nice for casual gaming and media, but they can cause echo in voice chat.

## Other peripherals worth considering

| Peripheral | Tip |
|---|---|
| **Mousepad** | Get a large "desk mat" (900×400mm) for low-sensitivity aim. A cloth pad gives control, and a hard or glass pad gives speed. This is a cheap and worthwhile upgrade. |
| **Microphone** | A USB mic is a big step up from a headset mic for streaming, calls and recording. Use a boom arm and a pop filter. |
| **Webcam** | 1080p at 30–60fps is fine for calls. Good lighting matters more than a better webcam. |
| **Controller** | An Xbox controller works natively on Windows. It's good for racing, sports and many single-player games. |
| **Speakers** | Powered bookshelf speakers are a big step up from cheap desktop speakers. |
| **Monitor arm** | Frees up desk space and gives full adjustment. The monitor needs VESA mount support. |
| **UPS** | A battery backup that protects the PC from power outages and surges. Recommended in areas with unstable power. |

---

**Quick verdict:**
- **Keyboard:** A **TKL or 75% mechanical** keyboard suits most people. Competitive FPS players should look at a **Hall effect** board with Rapid Trigger. For heavy spreadsheet or office work, keep a full-size board with a number pad.
- **Mouse:** Pick **shape and weight to fit your hand and grip first**. Any modern sensor is good enough, and wireless (2.4GHz) no longer has a downside.
- **Headset:** A **closed-back wired headset** like the HyperX Cloud III is the value pick, and **2.4GHz wireless** is worth it if you want no cable. Never game on Bluetooth audio.
- **Don't skip the cheap upgrades:** a large mousepad and a proper mic improve the experience more than they cost.
